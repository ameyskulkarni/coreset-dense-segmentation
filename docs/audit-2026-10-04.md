## 1. CRITICAL: at 5–10% data, rare-class mIoU is largely set by the training budget, not by image content

### What happens
Training uses a fixed number of epochs (ADR 0001), so the number of gradient steps scales
with the subset size. On Cityscapes that gives 3,700 steps at 10% and 1,800 at 5% (full data
gets 37,100). The ADR flagged this as an open question ("could look like 'random collapses at
5%' for reasons that are actually just undertraining") but nobody checked it. The data says
it happens.

**Evidence: per-class val IoU (%) of the random baseline, mean ± std over 3 seeds (from `runs.csv`)**

| ratio | steps | motorcycle | rider | traffic light | train | bus | rare mIoU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 1,800 | 9.5 ± 16.4 | **0.0 ± 0.0** | **0.7 ± 1.1** | **0.0 ± 0.0** | 13.7 ± 12.2 | 4.8 ± 5.5 |
| 0.10 | 3,700 | 19.9 ± 15.8 | 13.9 ± 7.6 | 32.4 ± 12.0 | **0.0 ± 0.0** | 35.5 ± 0.5 | 20.3 ± 5.0 |
| 0.20 | 7,400 | 35.0 ± 7.6 | 31.8 ± 1.7 | 49.4 ± 1.7 | 30.2 ± 4.0 | 46.9 ± 1.2 | 38.7 ± 1.4 |
| 1.00 | 37,100 | 56.4 ± 0.6 | 48.2 ± 0.2 | 61.3 ± 0.2 | 68.1 ± 0.5 | 77.9 ± 0.8 | 62.4 ± 0.1 |

**Evidence: how many training images in those subsets actually contain each class (computed from the labels)**

| subset | traffic light | bus | train | rider | motorcycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random 5%, seeds 0/1/2 (149 imgs) | 80 / 94 / 81 | 13 / 19 / 16 | 7 / 4 / 8 | 49 / 57 / 46 | 31 / 27 / 21 |
| random 10%, seeds 0/1/2 (298 imgs) | 163 / 187 / 165 | 30 / 31 / 28 | 13 / 14 / 14 | 101 / 106 / 102 | 54 / 55 / 54 |

At 5%, **one third of the training images (46–57 of 149) contain a rider, yet rider IoU is
exactly 0.0 in all three seeds**. **More than half (80–94) contain a traffic light, yet
traffic-light IoU is 0.7 ± 1.1.** For these two classes the model is not short of examples. It
simply hasn't started predicting small classes after 1,800 steps. At 10% the two causes are
mixed: `train` IoU is 0.0 in all seeds with 13–14 train images (that could be data scarcity
*or* budget), while traffic light (165–187 images) only reaches 32% IoU against 61% on full
data. (Segmentation nets typically learn small and rare classes late, and this recipe gives the
randomly initialized decode head no LR multiplier; see 1b.)

### Why it ruins results
- **The headline story is confounded.** "Rare-class mIoU degrades much faster than overall
  mIoU" (`docs/results.md`) and Fig. 1 (rare-class IoU vs ratio) would be presented as a
  data-selection phenomenon. At the low end they are mostly an optimization-budget
  phenomenon. A reviewer who asks "is this just undertraining?" sinks Contribution 1 and the
  mechanism (Contribution 2), and right now you cannot answer.
- **Floor effects remove discriminative power at the low end of the curve.** At 5%, rider IoU
  is 0 for every random seed even with ~50 rider images. If prototypicality (or
  `patch_coverage`) picks *more* rider images, rider IoU will likely still be ~0, because the
  model never learns the class within the budget. A rare-class-aware selector **may be unable
  to win at 5–10% even if its selection is better**. That would manufacture the exact negative
  result the paper is about.
- Selection methods compared at the *same* ratio do get the same number of steps, so the
  comparison is "fair". It is just measuring a regime where the metric is saturated at zero.

### 1b. Contributing cause: the recipe is probably under-tuned for short runs
- The LR sweep ({3e-5, 6e-5, 1e-4} → 68.8 / 71.4 / **72.8**) is monotone, and the winner sits
  at the **edge of the grid**, so the real optimum may be higher. It was tuned only at
  37,100 steps.
- The official SegFormer/mmseg recipe uses `lr_mult=10` on the decode head and
  `decay_mult=0` on norms/pos-blocks. Here the random-init head trains at 1e-4 with weight
  decay on everything (`trainer.py:95`). A slow-learning head hurts most at small step
  counts, which is exactly the low-ratio regime.

### Proposed fix (do this before launching the 45 prototypicality runs)
1. **Decisive check (~8 GPU-h):** train random 5% and random 10% (seeds 0–2) at a fixed
   37,100 steps (`--set recipe.epochs=2000 recipe.warmup_epochs=120` for 5%,
   `recipe.epochs=1000 recipe.warmup_epochs=60` for 10%; this keeps warmup at ~2,200 steps).
   If rider, traffic-light and train IoU recover substantially, the low-ratio rare-class
   collapse is a budget artifact.
2. If confirmed, make the diagnostic run on a **minimum step budget**: either fixed iterations
   as the primary protocol for Contributions 1–2 (keeping fixed-epoch results as the
   efficiency figure, Fig. 3), or a per-ratio step floor (the ADR's own suggested fix). Report
   both protocols. The ADR promised a fixed-iteration ablation anyway; it is now load-bearing.
3. Extend the LR sweep by one point (2e-4). Optionally add the mmseg head `lr_mult=10`. Then
   re-freeze. Do this once, on full data, as the plan requires.

## 2. HIGH: the planned statistics cannot produce a significant result

### What happens
Plan §7: "A method 'wins' only if its mean is outside random's ±1 std band... paired
comparison (e.g., Wilcoxon across seeds)". With **n = 3 seeds**:
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank with n = 3 cannot reach p < 0.05.** Its smallest attainable p-value
  is 0.25 (two-sided) or 0.125 (one-sided). With n = 5 it is 0.0625 (two-sided). The proposed
  test can never reject, whatever the data.
- "Outside ±1 std" with a std estimated from 3 points is a very unreliable criterion (the
  std's own 95% CI spans roughly 0.5× to 6× its point value). It is not a significance test,
  and reviewers know that.
- Rare-class variance is large and dominated by tiny val sample sizes: `train` appears in
  **22 of 500** val images, bus in 75. Seed std of rare mIoU is 5.0 at 10% and 2.1 at 50%,
  and single-class seed std reaches 11–16 points (motorcycle at 5%/10%, traffic light at 10%,
  bus at 5% and 30%).

### Why it ruins results
Every Table 1/3 claim ("fails to beat random", "dips below random on rare classes") rests on
this test. As written, the test can't support a positive *or* a negative claim. A negative
result ("selection doesn't beat random") especially needs power: "no significant difference
with n = 3" is not evidence of no effect.

### Proposed fix
- Use a **paired t-test or a linear mixed model** (`metric ~ method + ratio + (1|seed)`),
  pooled across ratios, and report effect sizes with 95% CIs. Reserve Wilcoxon for n ≥ 6.
- Add **image-level bootstrap CIs over the val set**: resample the 500 val images and
  recompute from per-image confusion matrices. This captures the 22-image `train` problem
  directly. It requires saving a per-image confusion matrix in `Evaluator.evaluate`, which is
  cheap: 19×19 ints per image.
- For the negative claim, use **equivalence testing (TOST)** with a pre-stated margin
  (e.g. ±1 mIoU).
- Go to 5 seeds for random *and* the headline method at the core ratios (plan §7 already asks
  for 5), and write the analysis plan down before looking at the prototypicality numbers.

## 3. HIGH: Boundary-F is not comparable across runs, and rescoring doesn't reach the tables

### What happens
`boundary_f_score` (`segcoreset/eval/metrics.py:97`) changed in commit `12cc5ed` (today). It
now excludes boundary pixels on or 8-adjacent to ignore regions, and the empty-GT handling
changed too. I confirmed this with a docstring-stripped AST diff between `16f3585` (random
baseline) and HEAD: this is the **only** semantic change in the train/eval path, so mIoU and
rare-mIoU stay comparable. But:
- All 35 rows in `runs.csv`, including the whole random baseline and the `Boundary F` column
  in `docs/results.md`, use the **old** definition (the docstring measured 0.434 old vs 0.509
  new on the same checkpoint). Every prototypicality run will use the **new** one, so a
  direct comparison would show a spurious ~+5–7 point "win" for every method.
- The docstring says "rescore old checkpoints with `scripts/04_eval.py`". But `04_eval.py`
  appends rows with `run_id=<name>_eval` and leaves `selection_method`, `ratio` and
  `n_images` **blank**. The comparison code in `run_cityscapes_prototypicality.sh` matches
  `^cs_<sel>_r<ratio>_s<seed>$`, so `_eval` rows are silently ignored and the stale
  old-definition values are what get tabulated.

### Proposed fix
- Rescore all random-baseline checkpoints (`results/checkpoints/cs_random_*`, plus the
  `cs_lrsweep_*` / `cs_disccheck_*` ones if you will cite them) before reporting any boundary
  number.
- Make `04_eval.py` copy `selection_method`, `ratio`, `n_images` and `seed` from the
  original ledger row (or from `config.yaml` plus the subset path, see #9). Add a
  `metric_version` column (e.g. `bf_v2_ignore_adjacent`) so aggregation can filter on it.
- Have aggregation prefer the latest `_eval` row per base run and metric version.

## 4. HIGH (upcoming Contribution 3/4): `kmeans_hist` pseudo-classes are badly fitted

### What happens
`scripts/00b_derive_embeddings.py::_kmeans_hist` streams patches into
`MiniBatchKMeans.partial_fit`, one image per call, in **sorted id order**. I confirmed the
following in the installed sklearn 1.7.2 source:
1. **All 256 centroids are initialized from the first `partial_fit` call only**, i.e. the
   1,369 patches of a single image (`aachen_000000_000019`). k-means++ over one street scene
   cannot place centroids for content that image doesn't contain.
2. `n_init=3` is **ignored** by `partial_fit`, so there is only one initialization.
3. There is a single pass, and the stream is **ordered by city** (aachen → … → zurich).
   MiniBatchKMeans uses per-centroid count-based learning rates that decay roughly as
   1/count, so early cities dominate the centroid positions and later cities only nudge them.
   The low-count reassignment (`reassignment_ratio`) only partly compensates.
4. `batch_size=8192` has no effect, because `partial_fit` uses whatever single image it
   receives.

### Why it ruins results
This derivation *is* the plan's §5.3 pseudo-class vocabulary. `patch_coverage` weights
pseudo-classes by `w_k = 1/freq(k)`, so a distorted vocabulary (city-biased, with rare
content merged into common centroids) directly corrupts the rarity weights the paper's method
depends on. It would also handicap the "dense representation" arm of Contribution 3. A failure
here would read as "dense/patch coverage doesn't help", when the real cause is a broken
clustering step.

### Proposed fix
Sample, say, 200 random patches per image with a fixed seed (~600k × 768 floats, ~1.8 GB in
float32, fine), shuffle, and use `MiniBatchKMeans(...).fit(sample)` with `n_init ≥ 3` and
several epochs, or use faiss k-means / full k-means on the sample. Then assign all patches.
Log cluster-size statistics (empty or near-empty clusters) and keep the sample seed in
`params`. Since the patches are L2-normalized, spherical k-means (already implemented in
`scoring.py`) is the consistent choice.

## 5. MEDIUM-HIGH: the "rare class" set doesn't match the mechanism you want to claim

### What happens
`results/rare_classes/cityscapes.json` holds the bottom-5 classes by **pixel** frequency:
motorcycle, rider, traffic light, train, bus. On the val set:

| class | val images containing it (of 500) |
|---|---|
| traffic light | **291** (58%) |
| rider | **253** (51%) |
| motorcycle | 93 |
| bus | 75 |
| truck (6th, just outside the cutoff) | 80 |
| train | **22** |

Traffic light and rider are *small but ubiquitous*. They are rare in pixels, not in images.
The cutoff is also razor-thin: bus 0.235% vs truck 0.267%, and train 0.233% vs bus 0.235%.

### Why it matters
Contribution 2's mechanism is "rare classes co-occur with common ones, so image-level
selection can't isolate them". That is an *image-frequency* argument. For classes present in
over half of all images, image-level selection is irrelevant almost by construction, so they
dilute or mask any selection effect on the genuinely rare classes (train, bus, motorcycle,
truck). `train`, with 22 val images, also makes the averaged metric very noisy (see #2).

### Proposed fix
Keep the frozen pixel-rare list (don't change it after seeing results). Also pre-specify a
second, **image-rare** set: bottom-K by number of training images containing the class
(computed from train labels, analysis only). Report both, plus the per-class table. Make the
mechanism argument on the image-rare set, and say explicitly that small-object classes are a
different failure mode.

## 6. MEDIUM: DINOv2 features come from aspect-distorted images (hurts the dense arm)

### What happens
`segcoreset/features/dino.py:57` does `T.Resize((518, 518))`. Cityscapes images are
1024×2048, so they are squashed **4× horizontally and 2× vertically**. Each patch token covers
~55×28 original pixels, so thin vertical objects (poles, traffic lights, riders) and small
objects get anisotropically compressed. `segb0_encoder.py` does the same.

### Why it matters
For the CLS-based prototypicality runs this is a mild, defensible choice. But the paper's
central contrast is **dense/patch vs global** (Contribution 3, and `patch_coverage` vs
`zcore`). A preprocessing step that specifically degrades small objects in the patch grid
biases that comparison *against* the dense representation, which is the side you're
arguing for.

### Proposed fix
Use aspect-preserving extraction for the patch representation: 518×1036 (a 37×74 grid;
DINOv2 interpolates position embeddings for non-square inputs), or two 518×518 tiles. Re-run
`00`/`00b` once and record the resolution in the `features` config name so subset names stay
distinct.

## 7. MEDIUM: the prototypicality grid risks a strawman

### What happens
`scripts/experiments/run_cityscapes_prototypicality.sh` runs **only** `prototypicality-hard`
(keep-hard, `balance: 0.5`) at keep ratios 0.7 → 0.1. `docs/prototypicality.md` §10 itself
says keep-easy "should run early", because Sorscher et al.'s theory says to **keep easy
examples when data is scarce**, and most of your grid (10–30% kept) is that regime. The
cluster-balanced variant is also a project invention: Sorscher's App. H balancing uses labels.
The faithful label-free version is the unbalanced `-nobal` preset, and it is not in the batch.

### Why it matters
If keep-hard loses to random at 10–20%, a reviewer will say you ran the canonical baseline in
the direction its own paper says is wrong for that regime, with a modification the paper
doesn't have. Plan §5.0 criterion 4 ("a botched reimplementation is worse than an omission")
applies directly.

### Proposed fix
At minimum add `prototypicality-easy` (k=20) and `prototypicality-hard-nobal` (k=20) at
ratios 0.1, 0.2, 0.3. Report the faithful unbalanced version as "prototypicality (Sorscher
et al.)" and the balanced one as your strengthened variant.

## 8. MEDIUM: training data order is non-deterministic, so same-seed runs don't reproduce

### What happens
`segcoreset/data/base.py:50-58` builds the training sample list as
`[by_id[i] for i in set(subset_ids)]`. Iteration order over a set of strings depends on the
process's hash seed, which is random per interpreter. `set_seed()` writes
`PYTHONHASHSEED` *after* startup, which has no effect on the current process (its own
docstring admits this). So with the same seed, the DataLoader's shuffled order differs from
run to run. The docstring documents this as "accepted": the bug was written up instead of
fixed, which looks like a leftover from repeated AI rewrites.

### Why it matters
- The "seed" doesn't fully determine training, so you can't claim bit-for-bit
  reproducibility.
- The plan's paired comparison (random seed *s* vs method seed *s*) assumes the training
  seed controls the same randomness on both sides. It doesn't. The measured rerun noise is
  small (~0.05 mIoU at r0.1), so this is not a big error source, but it is an easy
  methods-section vulnerability.

### Proposed fix
One line: `samples = [s for s in samples if s.image_id in subset_ids]` (keeps sorted path
order). This doesn't invalidate the existing random runs: their order was a uniformly random
permutation, which is statistically equivalent. Apply it before the prototypicality batch and
note the commit.

## 9. MEDIUM: provenance gaps in the ledger

1. **`config_hash` ignores the subset.** It hashes only the YAML config, so `995fe9bbcaf3` is
   shared by r1.0_s0, r0.9_s0, …, r0.05_s0, the LR-sweep 1e-4 run, and every future
   prototypicality seed-0 run. The hash cannot identify a run.
2. **The selection seed is never logged.** The ledger's `seed` column is the training seed.
   The subset seed lives only in the run name by convention, and the convention has already
   broken once: `cs_disccheck_random_r0.1_s1/_s2` trained with seed 0.
3. **Default run names collide.** Without `--run-name`, the name is
   `<ds>_<model>_<method>_r<ratio>_s<train-seed>_<hash>`. Subsets of seed 1 and seed 2
   trained with `--seed 0` get the *same* name, so the second run overwrites the first's
   `results/checkpoints/...` directory.
4. **`run_cityscapes_random_baseline.sh` passes `--allow-dirty`** ("other code is being
   edited while this batch runs"). Each `03_train.py` re-imports the working tree, so the 6
   runs `cs_random_r0.1_*` / `cs_random_r0.05_*` ran **uncommitted, unknown code**, and their
   `git_commit` certifies nothing. Indirect evidence that they are fine: `cs_random_r0.1_s0`
   (0.5266 / 0.5273) reproduces the committed-code run `cs_disccheck_random_r0.1_s0_lr1e-4`
   (0.5266) on the same subset and seed. Still, don't do this for any run you report.

**Fix:** add `subset_path`, `subset_sha256` (hash of the sorted id list) and `selection_seed`
columns, and fold `subset_sha256` into `config_hash`. Include the selection seed in the
default run name. Remove `--allow-dirty` from the launcher. Optionally re-run the 6 dirty
runs once the code is frozen (~2 GPU-h).

## 10. MEDIUM: the docs contradict the frozen recipe (leftovers from repeated rewrites)

**Fix:** add an ADR amendment recording 100 epochs, 6 warmup epochs and lr 1e-4. Mark the

run's `results/checkpoints/<run>/config.yaml` instead of from the prose docs.

## 11. LOW-MEDIUM: uncalibrated recipes for later stages

- **Stage 4 (DeepLabV3+ / U-Net):** the same AdamW lr=1e-4 SegFormer recipe would be applied
  to ResNet CNNs, which are usually trained with SGD at lr≈0.01. A badly tuned CNN makes
  "the coreset transfers across architectures" weak either way. Run a minimal full-data LR
  check per architecture, once, before Stage 4.
- **ADE20K:** `ade20k_proxy` keeps lr 6e-5 and 8 epochs, and the warmup is 12.5% of the
  schedule. It needs its own sweep and its own discriminativeness check, *including the
  low-ratio budget check from #1*: at 10% ADE20K, 8 epochs is only ~2,000 steps.

## 12. What I checked and found correct

These are fine, and you can rely on them:
- **Cityscapes label mapping:** the labelId → trainId LUT matches cityscapesScripts for all 19
  classes, and void/license-plate map to 255.
- **mIoU, pixel accuracy, rare mIoU:** the confusion matrix accumulates over the whole val
  set, standard dataset-level IoU, with ignore pixels excluded and NaN only when a class is
  absent from both GT and prediction.
- **Sliding window:** a 3×3 grid of tiles at y ∈ {0, 341, 512}, x ∈ {0, 683, 1024},
  identical to mmseg's grid formula. Logits are averaged before the argmax, and zero-padding
  in normalized space equals mean-pixel padding.
- **Epoch → step conversion, warmup and poly schedule** (`trainer.py`, `lr_schedule.py`). A
  one-step LR lag exists but is negligible.
- **DataLoader worker seeding** (`_seed_worker`, spawn context) and AMP/GradScaler usage.
- **SegFormer weight loading:** only the classifier is UNEXPECTED and only the decode head is
  MISSING, so the encoder is loaded. Full-data 72.6 mIoU is plausible for B0 at 37k steps.
- **No label leakage into selection:** labels are used only in `01_compute_rare_classes.py`
  and in eval.
- **Random selector:** uniform without replacement, independent RNG, sorted input order.
- **Prototypicality implementation** (`scoring.py`, `pruning.py`, `selector.py`): k-means++
  sampling ∝ (1−cos) ≡ ∝ squared Euclidean on the unit sphere; spherical Lloyd updates;
  empty-cluster reseeding; best-of-n_init by total cosine distance; quotas
  `floor(b·n_c·B/N)` provably sum to ≤ B; exact-budget assert; deterministic tie-breaking.
  Embedding rows are aligned to `image_ids` by id, not by position.
- **Subset reproducibility:** I re-ran `02_select.py` for three committed prototypicality
  subsets (k10/0.1/s2, k50/0.7/s0, k20/0.2/s1) and one random subset. All four reported
  "Subset unchanged" (identical ids), and the tree stayed clean.
- **Train/eval code is identical between the random baseline and HEAD**, apart from
  Boundary-F (#3). I checked with an AST diff that ignores docstrings, so random-vs-method
  comparisons on mIoU and rare-mIoU are not confounded by code drift.
